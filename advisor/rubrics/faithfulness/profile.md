Faithfulness to the profile: what the guidance says about this person and their group is in the prompt.

The prompt has the group profile (summary, focus areas, group deviations), the person's table
(each measure against the group mean and against everyone, in standard deviations), the line
on what stands out from the group, and the fixed flags. Grade every claim about the person or
the group against them. Direction matters more than the exact figure.

Score from 1 to 5:
5 = every claim matches the table in direction and rough size; "typical" is used only when nothing stands out; the recommendations address the focus areas and the measures where the person is worse than the group; no fact about the person that the prompt does not contain
4 = one imprecision that changes nothing: a rounded value, a measure called "low" when it is only slightly below the group
3 = one claim contradicts the table (sleep called short when it is above the group), or the person stands out and is called typical, or a measure where the person is better than the group is treated as a gap
2 = two or more contradictions, or an invented fact about the person: a habit, a job detail, a cause for a value
1 = nothing in the guidance comes from the table or the focus areas; it could have been written for anyone

Examples, unrelated to the case you are grading:
- Table: Sleep Duration 6.0 vs group 6.1 (-0.2 SD), Daily Steps 4200 vs group 5200 (-0.9 SD). Stands out: Daily Steps -0.9. Summary "your sleep is much shorter than others in your group". Score 3: sleep is at the group level, the gap is steps.
- Every measure within 0.3 SD of the group, stands out "nothing". Summary "you are typical of your group", recommendations on the three focus areas. Score 5.
- Recommendation "since your night shifts break up your sleep, ..." with no shift work anywhere in the prompt. Score 2.
- Summary "your stress is above your group's" when the table says Stress Level +0.4 SD vs group. Score 5: small but in the right direction.

Quote the unsupported claim in the reason. Answer with the integer score as the score field.
